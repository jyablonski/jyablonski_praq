# Guitar Amp Types

Amp type can mean how an amp works, how it sounds, or how it’s packaged. For heavy rhythm, listen for the snap of palm mutes and how clearly individual notes come through.

## Circuits and Formats

- **Tube (valve).** Tubes amplify the guitar signal in the preamp and drive the speaker in the power amp. Modern high-gain amps get most of their distortion from the preamp, even at low master volume.
- **Analog solid-state.** Transistor-based circuitry covers everything from the clean Roland Jazz Chorus to the aggressive Randall RG-100 that Dimebag Darrell used with Pantera.
- **Hybrid.** Mixes technologies. The Orange Micro Terror pairs a tube preamp with a solid-state power amp.
- **Digital.** A model recreates an amp’s behavior with adjustable controls, as in Fractal Axe-Fx and Line 6 Helix. A capture or profile starts with a measurement of a physical rig at a particular setting, as in Neural DSP Quad Cortex or Kemper Profiler.

A **combo** houses the amp and speaker together. A **head** connects to a separate speaker cabinet. A **modeling processor or plugin** can run direct with a simulated cabinet.

## Amp Families

| Family | What to listen for |
| --- | --- |
| Fender Deluxe Reverb / Twin Reverb | Clear, bright cleans and spring reverb. The Deluxe breaks up sooner. The Twin stays clean at higher volume. |
| Vox AC15 / AC30 | Chiming upper mids, especially through the Top Boost channel. |
| Roland JC-120 | Crisp solid-state cleans with a wide stereo chorus. |
| Marshall Super Lead / JCM800 2203 | Open, biting crunch. A Plexi-style Super Lead gets dirty as the volume rises. The 2203 has a master volume to set loudness independently of preamp gain. |
| Laney Supergroup | Raw, loud British crunch. Tony Iommi paired an early Supergroup with a treble booster for his Black Sabbath sound. |
| Orange Rockerverb | Thick mids and a grainy overdrive that works for sludge, stoner rock, and metal. The MkIII has a footswitchable attenuator. |
| Soldano SLO-100 | Smooth sustain with a firm pick attack. The 1987 design is an early reference for modern high gain. |
| Peavey 5150 / 6505 | Grainy, saturated rhythm crunch. Peavey launched the original 5150 with Eddie Van Halen in 1992 and continued that line as the 6505. |
| EVH 5150III | A distinct three-channel design with clean, crunch, and lead. The blue crunch channel can handle heavy rhythm on its own. |
| Mesa/Boogie Mark IIC+, IV, V, JP-2C | Tight attack and clear notes under heavy gain. On the JP-2C, keep the bass knob low and add weight with the graphic EQ later in the signal chain. |
| Mesa/Boogie Dual / Triple Rectifier | Broad low end and a coarse grind. With selectable rectification, silicon diodes give a firmer response and tube rectification softens the attack when pushed. |
| Diezel VH4 / Herbert | Dense gain with clear chord separation. VH4 channel 3 is the heavy rhythm reference. The Herbert adds a switchable mid cut. |
| ENGL Fireball / Powerball | Focused low end and sharp pick attack. The Powerball II has a built-in noise gate. |
| Bogner Uberschall | Thick saturation and deep bass. The presence control can bring the mids forward. |
| Friedman BE-100 | Hot-rodded Marshall-style crunch with extra saturation on the HBE channel. |
| Revv Generator | A tight, percussive Purple channel and a more saturated Red channel on the 120. |

## Rigs in Practice

- **Tool · Adam Jones.** On *Fear Inoculum*, Joe Barresi blended a Diezel VH4, a Marshall Super Bass, and a track combining a Bogner Uberschall with a Rivera Knucklehead. The Marshall brought out the pick attack and the Bogner added weight.
- **Dream Theater · John Petrucci.** Petrucci recorded *The Astonishing* with his signature JP-2C, a Mark IIC+ design with three dedicated channels and two graphic EQs.
- **Slipknot · Jim Root.** His 2015 live rig ran a Rockerverb 100 into an isolated Orange 4×12 with Vintage 30s. He later used a Headfirst Alta 100 live. By 2024, he was using a Quad Cortex for effects alongside his heads and for amp sounds at some shows.
- **Machine Head · Robb Flynn.** For *Unto the Locust*, Flynn blended a Peavey 5150 and a Marshall. He credits lower gain and consistent picking for keeping the riffs clear.

Metallica’s 2013 Antarctica show helped set up its transition to Axe-Fx rigs, which simplified shipping and made the sound more consistent. By 2022, Trivium had returned to 5150-style heads after years with Fractal and Kemper.

## Cabinets, Speakers, and Recording

- **Cabinet.** A 4×12 has four 12-inch speakers. Closed-back cabs generally sound more directional, with a firmer low end. Open-back cabs spread sound behind the amp and tend to feel airier in the room.
- **Speaker.** A Celestion Vintage 30 has prominent upper mids that help riffs cut through. A G12M Greenback has a softer top end and a more vintage crunch. Check the speaker model even when two cabinets have the same layout.
- **Direct recording.** A cab impulse response (IR) approximates the sound of a particular speaker, cabinet, and mic setup. Use cab simulation for a direct signal to a PA or recording interface. For silent recording from a tube head’s speaker output, use a load box with the correct impedance and power rating.

To compare amp voices, keep the cabinet or IR fixed and match playback levels. If palm mutes smear, try less bass or gain going into distortion, then adjust the low end after distortion.
